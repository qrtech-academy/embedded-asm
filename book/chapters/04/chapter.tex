\chapter{Pointers, Arrays and Structs in SRAM}
\label{c4:ch:pointers}

{\large\itshape Data structures with no compiler to lay them out for you.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{X}, \code{Y} and \code{Z}: three 16-bit pointers made out of six 8-bit registers.
  \item \code{ld}, \code{st}, and the post-increment and pre-decrement forms that make a walk one
    instruction.
  \item \code{ldd} and \code{std}: displacement addressing, and why \code{X} does not have it.
  \item \code{lpm}, and the address space no ordinary load can reach.
  \item Laying a structure out by hand: offsets, size, and what ``passing by reference'' costs.
  \item Arrays of structures, and the stride that is not one byte.
  \item Where in SRAM your structures should actually live, and why \code{RAMEND + 1} is not it.
  \item The stack, now that an interrupt can land on top of two nested calls.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

The chapter starts with six registers and three pointers. Work out, before you are told, what
distinguishes \code{ld r16, Z} from \code{ld r16, Z+} in the value it returns. The answer is
nothing, and that is the point. Then a structure is seven bytes: not a type, not a record, but
seven bytes at an address, and the only thing that makes them a structure is that you and your code
agree on the offsets. Then an array is a stride: walking one means adding the \emph{structure's}
size to a pointer, which is not one and is not a power of two, and that turns out to matter.

Then where to put it: SRAM is 2048 bytes shared between your variables and the stack, and nothing
on the device notices when they meet. And finally how deep the stack goes, a number you can
compute in advance, and this chapter's cross-check is computing it and then measuring it.

Finish Chapter~3 first. This chapter's array is an array of the LED structures Chapter~2 defined,
and the stack arithmetic counts Chapter~3's handler.

Two predictions are worth making before anything runs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item how many bytes of stack Chapter~3's program uses at its worst moment.
  \item what happens if you store a structure at \code{RAMEND + 1}.
\end{itemize}
The first is the cross-check. The second is a trap that a great deal of published AVR code falls
into.
