\chapter{I/O Ports and Subroutines}
\label{c2:ch:ports}

{\large\itshape The first driver, and the contract that makes a subroutine reusable.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{DDRx}, \code{PORTx} and \code{PINx}: three registers per port, and what each of the
    three actually does, which is more than Chapter~1's two \code{sbi} instructions had to know.
  \item The internal pull-up, which is a resistor you did not fit and cannot see.
  \item Writing a one to \code{PINx}, which toggles the output and is not what the name suggests at
    all.
  \item Arduino pin numbers against port bits, and why that translation is your driver's first
    job.
  \item \code{rcall} and \code{ret}: what goes on the stack, what comes back off it, and what it
    costs.
  \item The calling contract: which registers a subroutine may destroy and which it must give
    back.
  \item The LED driver: five subroutines, a structure in SRAM, and a cost that depends on the pin.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

The chapter starts with one pin and its four states. \code{DDRx} and \code{PORTx} are two bits, so
there are four combinations, and each of them does something different; predict what a floating
input reads before you are told. Then the pull-up, and the button that reads backwards: a pressed
button reads zero, which is not a convention anybody chose but falls out of the circuit, and the
circuit is drawn. Then the translation: Arduino pin 13 is bit 5 of port B, and the mapping is worth
deriving rather than reading. Then subroutines, properly: what \code{rcall} pushes, what
\code{ret} does not undo, and the register contract that makes a subroutine safe to call from code
you did not write. And finally the driver: five subroutines over a structure in SRAM, so that two
LEDs can exist without the driver knowing there are two.

This chapter calls \code{shift_bits}, and the LED driver does not work without it, so finish
Chapter~1 first.

Two predictions are worth making before anything runs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item what \code{led_on} costs for Arduino pin 13, given what you already know \code{shift_bits}
    costs.
  \item whether \code{led_enabled} takes the same time whether the LED is on or off.
\end{itemize}
The second is this chapter's cross-check, and the answer is no.
