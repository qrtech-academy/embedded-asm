\chapter{Watchdog, Sleep and the C ABI}
\label{c6:ch:abi}

{\large\itshape Finishing the library, and connecting it to the language everything else is
written in.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What a watchdog protects against, and the much longer list of what it does not.
  \item The timed write sequence: hardware that refuses to be configured carelessly, and why.
  \item The one typo in this chapter that leaves a device resetting every sixteen milliseconds.
  \item Sleep modes, and which of them a timer can wake you from. It is fewer than you would like.
  \item The AVR C ABI in full, and where you have been following it since Chapter~1 without a
    second party.
  \item Calling your assembly from C, and reading what the compiler generates for the same job.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

The chapter starts with what a watchdog is for. Read \secref{c6:sec:detects} and decide, before
you are told, which of five failures a watchdog would catch. The answer is two of them, and a third
eventually. Then the timed sequence: two writes, four cycles apart at most. Get it wrong and the
register holds a value that is not merely wrong but specifically dangerous.

Then sleeping: what stops in each mode, and the two things that can always wake you. Then the
other party to the contract. \Secref{c6:sec:abi} is the one place in this book where somebody
else's code has to agree with yours about registers, and it turns out you have been getting it
right since Chapter~1. And finally the capstone: call \code{shift_bits}, unchanged from
Chapter~1, from a C program, then read what the compiler produces for the same job and compare.

Finish Chapter~5 first. The watchdog is configured from a table of scattered bits, in the same way
the timer was configured from a prescaler and a compare value.

Two predictions are worth making before anything runs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item whether a watchdog would have caught the atomicity bug from Chapter~3.
  \item how many instructions the compiler needs for \code{shift_bits} against your nine, and what
    its cost comes to against your $6n + 10$.
\end{itemize}
The second is this chapter's cross-check, and the honest answer is not the flattering one.
